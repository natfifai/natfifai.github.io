Here's a number that should scare every freelancer: **40% of self-employed workers underpay their taxes**, and nearly the same percentage overpay because they miss deductions.

Both problems come from the same place: not tracking your money properly.

You don't need QuickBooks. You don't need a bookkeeper (yet). You need a system that takes 5 minutes per week and gives you complete financial clarity. Here's how to build it.

## Why Most Freelancers Fail at Financial Tracking

The pattern is always the same:

1. You start freelancing and money comes in
2. You spend some of it
3. Tax season arrives
4. You panic, dig through bank statements for 3 days, and pray you didn't miss anything

Sound familiar? The fix isn't discipline — it's **a system so simple you'll actually use it.**

## The 8 Things Every Freelancer Should Track

### 1. Income (Every Dollar In)

For every payment you receive, log:
- **Date** — when the money hit your account
- **Client** — who paid you
- **Project** — what the payment was for
- **Amount** — how much
- **Invoice number** — for matching payments to invoices
- **Status** — paid, pending, or overdue

**Pro tip:** Log income when you *receive* it, not when you invoice it. Cash basis accounting is simpler and what most freelancers use for taxes.

### 2. Expenses (Every Dollar Out)

For every business expense:
- **Date and vendor** — when and where
- **Amount** — how much
- **Category** — software, marketing, travel, etc.
- **Tax deductible?** — flag this NOW, not in April
- **Receipt?** — mark whether you have one

### 3. Client Revenue Totals

Which client makes you the most money? Which one pays the least per hour of work? You should know this instantly.

Track total revenue per client, number of projects, and average project value. This tells you who to nurture and who to fire.

### 4. Invoices and Payment Status

Nothing kills cash flow like unpaid invoices you forgot about. Track:
- Every invoice sent
- Due date
- Status (sent, paid, overdue)
- Days outstanding

**Rule of thumb:** If an invoice is 7+ days past due, follow up. If it's 30+ days, you have a problem.

### 5. Tax Set-Aside

The freelancer's worst enemy: spending money that belongs to the IRS.

**Set aside 25-30% of every payment immediately.** Not at the end of the month. Not at the end of the quarter. The moment money hits your account, move 25% to a separate bucket.

### 6. Profit Margin

Revenue is vanity. **Profit is reality.**

If you made $8,000 this month but spent $3,000 on tools, contractors, and ads, your profit is $5,000. Your margin is 62.5%. Track this monthly.

### 7. Monthly P&L (Profit & Loss)

A month-by-month view of revenue, expenses, and profit. This shows you:
- Seasonal patterns (slow months vs. busy months)
- Whether your business is growing or shrinking
- Where you're overspending

### 8. Quarterly Tax Estimates

If you owe more than $1,000 in taxes for the year, the IRS expects quarterly payments. Your tracking system should automatically summarize:
- Quarterly income
- Quarterly deductible expenses
- Estimated tax due

## The 5-Minute Weekly Routine

Every Friday (or whatever day works):

1. **Open your tracker** (2 seconds)
2. **Log any new income** (1 minute)
3. **Log any new expenses** (2 minutes)
4. **Check invoice status** — anyone overdue? (1 minute)
5. **Glance at your dashboard** — profit up or down? (30 seconds)

That's it. Five minutes. Do this consistently and tax season becomes a non-event.

## Common Freelancer Tax Deductions You're Probably Missing

- **Home office** — percentage of rent/mortgage based on office square footage
- **Internet** — percentage used for business
- **Software subscriptions** — any tool you use for work
- **Professional development** — courses, books, conferences
- **Health insurance premiums** — if you're self-employed
- **Mileage** — driving to client meetings
- **Bank and payment processing fees** — PayPal, Stripe, etc.

**The key:** Mark expenses as deductible *when you log them*, not at tax time. Future-you will be grateful.
